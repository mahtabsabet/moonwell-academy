## Altar tools

The four "elemental" tools are the backbone of Wiccan altar practice. The
common assignment is **Pentacle → Earth**, **Chalice → Water**, and **Wand**
and **Athame → Fire / Air**. The wand/athame split is genuinely contested:
Gardnerian Wicca tends to give the **athame to Fire** and the **wand to Air**,
while Golden-Dawn-influenced traditions swap them. We note the ambiguity in the
item text instead of asserting one.

| Item | Grounding |
|---|---|
| **Pentacle** | A disc (paten) symbolic of the element **Earth**; used to consecrate tools and offer protection. |
| **Chalice** | The ritual cup, tied to **Water**, blessing, and offering. |
| **Athame** | A ritual blade for directing energy — **Fire** in Gardnerian Wicca, **Air** in other systems. |
| **Wand** | Directs will/energy; commonly **Fire** (or **Air**, depending on tradition). |
| **Censer / Incense Burner** | Associated with **Air**; ritual censing "banishes evil" and makes the space inviting to welcome spirits. |
| **Besom** | The witch's broom, used to **sweep an area of negative energy** (often swept above the floor, symbolically). |
| **Ritual Bell** | Rung to clear stagnant energy and mark the phases of a rite. |
| **Cauldron** | A vessel of transformation, a classic altar/hearth tool. |
| **Salt Dish** | Salt for cleansing and casting protective circles. |
| **Offering Bowl** | Holds food/flower offerings left for spirits or deities. |

> **Note on smoke-cleansing:** in-game herb bundles are **garden herbs**
> (rosemary, mugwort, lavender) for cooking, brewing, and smoke-cleansing — we
> deliberately avoid "smudging" / white sage, which is a closed Indigenous
> practice, not generic witchcraft.

## Scrying mirror

A **scrying mirror** is a dark, reflective surface used for divination; **black
obsidian** is the most traditional material. The most famous European example
is **John Dee's obsidian mirror** (court astrologer to Elizabeth I) — recently
shown by geochemical analysis to be **Aztec obsidian from Pachuca, Mexico**, a
reminder that the black-mirror tradition runs through Mesoamerican as well as
European practice.

## Wall charms & hangings

| Item | Grounding |
|---|---|
| **Horseshoe** | Hung over a door for luck/protection. Orientation is a real folk dispute: **ends-up** "to hold the luck in" (common in Britain/Ireland) vs **ends-down** "to pour blessings out" (common across much of Europe/the Mediterranean). |
| **Witch's Ladder** | A charm of **knotted cord with feathers woven in**, the number of knots/charms set to the intention. The first recorded example — "rope with feathers" — was found in a Wellington, Somerset house demolished in **1878**, and now hangs in the **Pitt Rivers Museum**. |
| **Protective Wreath** | A woven ring of herbs/greenery hung at the threshold (folk protective/welcoming decor). |
| **Drying Herbs** | Bundles of garden herbs hung to dry — practical (cooking/brewing) and for smoke-cleansing. |
| **Moon Phase Hanging / Celestial Tapestry / Star Chart / Pressed Botanical** | Modern witchy/celestial home decor — presented as decoration, not claimed as old tradition. |
